A Letter

A personal note written with admiration, gratitude, and hope.

For Stacy — on this day

Dear Stacy,

There are so many ways to try and say what you mean to someone. I spent time thinking about this letter not because there wasn't anything to say, but because there was too much — and I wanted to say it well. On your birthday I want to slow down, notice, and tell you what I see when I think of you.

You have a steady warmth about you that doesn't ask for notice and rarely announces itself. People feel seen when you're around. You listen with patience, and by simply paying attention you make ordinary moments feel important. That is a rare and beautiful gift.

Your curiosity gives you courage. You keep asking questions even when answers are inconvenient. You try, you fail, and you try again — not because you must, but because you genuinely want to understand. That determination will carry you farther than you imagine. I believe in the work you do and the attention you bring to the world.

Grace for yourself, on the hard days: remember that growth is uneven. You will have doubts; everyone does. But those doubts are not a measure of your worth. They are a sign you are engaged, that you care enough to worry. Be kind to yourself — as kind as you are to others — and give yourself the patience you offer the people you love.

I admire the way you keep kindness in the foreground. In a world that often rewards the loudest voice, you choose to be generous and quiet, to help without ceremony, to offer steadiness. Those choices ripple far beyond what you can see.

On this birthday I want to celebrate you: for who you are, for who you're becoming, and for how you make other people better simply by being present. Keep your curiosity. Keep your openness. Keep your humor. These are the parts of you that shape small days into meaningful years.

Thank you for the little things — the thoughtful questions, the listening, the gestures that arrive without fanfare. Thank you for showing up. I hope the year ahead brings quiet confidence, joyful surprises, and the kind of friendships that steady and excite you at once.

Happy birthday, Stacy. I am glad you exist, and I can't wait to see the chapters you write next.

With admiration and care,
Someone who believes in you

P.S. Keep going. The best parts are still ahead.
